Recovery (120 members at maturity) uses the pool, 13-person outdoor co-ed cold plunge, saunas, world-class co-ed steam room, and gym — none of which consume court time. This tier is scalable. Club (60 members at maturity) is the pickleball tier. This number is hard-capped by court availability. Going above 60 overwhelms the court and starts eating into premium revenue hours.

Miami Reality Check

Why an all-weather enclosure is non-negotiable.

Miami summer highs average 90°F+ from June through September with 70–80% humidity. A glass enclosure traps heat — unventilated, interior temps hit 110°F+ by midday. That's heat-stroke territory, not a premium club experience. If we're building an enclosure, it needs AC, or it needs no roof.

DUA solves this with mini-split AC + ventilation and a fixed insulated roof, protecting year-round play. There is no staged fallback in this proposal — a closed roof without AC breaks the product, and an open roof weakens the club's premium promise.

Residential-Neighborhood Design Gate

Light can be contained. Pickleball impact noise must be engineered and field-tested.

This court should be designed as an acoustic enclosure, not a glass pavilion: residence-facing laminated acoustic glazing and solid wall assemblies, internally absorptive ceiling and wall treatments, sound-lock entry, vibration isolation at the steel and HVAC connections, and USA Pickleball Quiet Category equipment for all recreational play. Mesh alone does not contain impact noise.

Do not promise 24/7 play. Motorized blackout shades and fully shielded, downward-aimed, dimmable LEDs can control visible light spill, but curtains do not solve paddle/ball impact noise. The responsible base operating model is 7 AM–10 PM, with any later hours only after an independent acoustical study, photometric study, final permitting, and at-residence field testing confirm compliance. The design target is no measurable nuisance at neighboring residences — not just a beautiful enclosure.

Illustrative range: ~$5.0M (at 8% cap) → ~$6.7M (at 6% cap). Subject to underwriting, final hotel departmental margins, and executed membership performance.

The membership roster is a transferable asset.

180 memberships with annual contracts, a waitlist, and recurring dues is exactly the kind of asset that compresses cap rates. Buyers underwrite it. Lenders underwrite it. It signals DUA isn't just a 124-key boutique — it's a community with cashflow.

Dedicated rooftop pickleball inventory is emerging in Brickell. DUA's advantage is a fully enclosed, climate-controlled court integrated with a hotel-owned recovery stack and hospitality P&L — an operating asset that is materially harder to replicate than a standalone court.

Quick note on cap rates

Cap rate is the yield a buyer demands. Lower cap = buyer is willing to pay more for the same NOI. Higher cap = buyer demands more yield. Brickell boutique hotels trade at the lower end because they're high-demand assets.

NOI ÷ cap rate = property value. So ~$403K NOI ÷ 6% = ~$6.7M. ÷ 7% = ~$5.8M. ÷ 8% = ~$5.0M. Lower cap = higher value.

Owner Upside Case · Not the Base Underwrite

There is a credible path back toward ~$7.1M of value — without overselling court time.

The base case is ~180 members and ~$403K of Year 3 incremental NOI. The upside case adds 30 Recovery-only memberships (no additional court credits), grows sponsorship from ~$78K to ~$100K, and builds a year-round corporate wellness / group-buyout calendar. Those are high-margin levers that can add roughly $90K of annual NOI once mature.

That moves the illustrative Year 3 NOI toward ~$494K, or ~$7.1M at a 7% cap rate. It is presented as upside because it requires sales execution, recovery-area capacity validation, sponsor demand, and a permitted operating schedule. The base case stays disciplined at ~$5.8M.

Why this stacks with wellness events like PNC

Wellness events create discovery, social content, and a qualified audience that already values movement, recovery, and community. The pickleball club converts that event traffic into recurring memberships, F&B, spa bookings, and return visits. PNC is one example; the strategy works with a broader calendar of aligned wellness events.

Why the location is still defensible

Within the nearby Brickell core, the existing rooftop courts are private or open-air, not professional climate-controlled venues with real wind, acoustic, and light-control infrastructure. In a dense 2–3 mile high-rise corridor, a new court requires rooftop capacity, structural approvals, an acoustic strategy, light containment, and a hospitality operating platform. DUA already owns the recovery stack that makes this build worth doing.

Why Founding prepayment matters

Founding rates create urgency, cash before launch, and the first advocates. Members prepay Year 1 to lock the Founding rate and avoid the initiation fee. Later tiers prove willingness to pay at higher pricing. The real value is not the court alone; it is the contracted recurring-revenue asset attached to DUA.

Why the recovery stack is the moat

Not every hotel can add a court and pair it with a pool, 13-person outdoor co-ed cold plunge, saunas, world-class co-ed steam room, gym, towel service, spa, and F&B — already on-property and already staffed. The amenities DUA already owns make membership worth $299, not $80.
